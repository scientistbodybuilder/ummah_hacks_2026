# Chunk 12: Maysir (Gambling/Speculation) & Gharar — BNPL Encouragement of Debt Traps

## 1-Sentence Rule
**Maysir** (gambling/speculation) is **strictly Haram**; BNPL/fintech products that **encourage unplanned spending or trap users in escalating debt cycles** violate this principle, even if individual transactions are technically Halal.

## What to Check
- Does the app **encourage "swipe now, pay later"** behavior with minimal friction (red flag: designed to trigger impulse)?
- Can users **exceed their financial means** without affordability checks (e.g., no income verification)?
- Does the app **gamify purchasing** (e.g., "rewards," "cashback," "point systems") to incentivize more BNPL use?
- Is there **aggressive marketing** (e.g., notifications, pop-ups, social proof) that normalizes debt?
- Does the app **enable users to stack multiple BNPL purchases**, creating un-transparent total debt?
- Is there **no debt-limit enforcement** (user can borrow indefinitely)?
- Does the interface **hide total cost/commitment** (e.g., buried in small print)?
- Are there **no financial literacy resources** or warnings about debt risks?

## Red Flag Keywords
- Buy now, pay later, instant approval, no income check
- Unlimited purchasing power, no credit limit, borrow as much as you want
- Rewards, cashback, loyalty points, stack your purchases
- Swipe instantly, one-tap checkout, frictionless
- Social proof (e.g., "1 million users," "trending," "celebrities use this")
- Interest-free, but (buried caveats on late fees)

## Questions to Ask If Uncertain
1. Does the **affordability assessment verify income**, or just ask "can you afford this?" (trust-based)?
2. Can a user with **₱20,000/month income borrow ₱50,000 across multiple BNPL apps**?
3. Is there a **hard debt limit** (e.g., max ₱100,000 borrowed), or unlimited?
4. If a user **defaults on multiple BNPL apps**, is the risk shared across lenders or siloed?
5. Does the interface **prominently display total outstanding debt** across all BNPL purchases?
6. Are **warning messages shown** (e.g., "You are now at X% of your income in debt")?
7. Is there a **financial counseling service** for users in distress?

## Shariah Status
- **Verdict (Haram - Maysir)**: App encourages unplanned spending, debt traps, or gambling-like behavior; individual transactions may be Halal but system-level design is Haram
- **Verdict (Conditional)**: Product is Halal if affordability is enforced, debt limits exist, and marketing is ethical
- **Confidence Level**: High (OIC Fiqh Academy, Maqasid Al-Shariah principle of preserving wealth)

## Evidence & Citations
- **OIC Fiqh Academy**: "Maysir (gambling/speculation) is strictly prohibited. Products that encourage uncontrolled debt or speculation violate Islamic principles regardless of individual transaction structure."
- **BNM Policy (2024)**: "BNPL platforms must conduct affordability assessments; aggressive marketing that encourages debt is non-compliant."
- **Malaysia SAC on BNPL Ethics**: "Gambling-like encouragement of shopping (Maysir) is separate from Shariah compliance of individual transactions; a fully Halal BNPL structure can be unethical if system design traps users in debt."
- **RSSIS Journal (2025)**: "BNPL apps Atome, Shopee, Lazada encourage 'unlimited shopping' without hard debt limits; ethical concern for Maysir-like behavior, especially targeting youth."
- **Maqasid Al-Shariah Principle**: Preservation of wealth (Hifz Al-Mal) requires protecting consumers from destructive debt cycles, even if transactions are individually Halal.
- **Islamic Finance Singapore**: "BNPL platforms must demonstrate responsible lending; frictionless purchasing without affordability checks is ethically non-compliant."
